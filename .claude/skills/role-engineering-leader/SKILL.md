# Role preset: engineering leader

Fit: teaching posts and substantive comments that make strong peers want to talk to you, plus finding those peers. The measure is conversations you found interesting, not reach.

## Questions (after the common interview)
1. What do you build, and what can you explain better than most? 3 topics.
2. Who do you want to meet? Push for a bar, e.g. "builders with 8+ years, founders, research leads who publish". Ask for 3 real examples.
3. What would make a conversation "interesting" for you? (A specific thing you'd learn, a collaboration, a hire, an idea.)
4. What is internal and must stay out (employer internals, metrics, customers)? What framing is OK in public?
5. Events or communities you go to offline.
6. Hours a week (default 3–5).

## What to write
- **North star**: *interesting conversations per week*. Set only on the user's word (`interesting: true`).
- **Scorecard**:
  - impressions (7 days);
  - comments from others per 1,000 impressions at day 7;
  - substantive replies;
  - person touches and the reply rate;
  - interesting conversations (★);
  - hours.
- **Calibre bar**: their bar, verbatim, with the 3 examples.
- **Outreach stages**: `sent → replied → met | no_response`, plus `interesting: true/false`.
- **FOCUS.md**:
  - online presence → conversations;
  - their current deep-work theme (e.g. learning or a talk);
  - one event or person spike.
- **Jobs on**:
  - `scout` (weekly: people and events in their city, with one thing to talk about each);
  - `inspiration` (daily: posts worth a substantive reply);
  - `digest` (daily);
  - `weekly-review`.

## Drafting rules for this role
- A post teaches one mechanism a senior engineer can use without clicking the link. Opinionated, not storytelling.
- Messages: the specific thing of theirs first, one real thing about you, one question. Under 60 words.
- Never claim expertise or metrics the user hasn't stated.

## Limits to tell the user
- Reach is a leading signal only. Judge it on conversations, at 4 weeks and again at 8.
- Offline events often beat posts for conversations. The scout surfaces events too.
